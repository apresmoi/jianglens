# Monad

**Aliases:** the Monad, divine source, universal consciousness, connected consciousness, source of creativity.

> **Fast answer:** Jiang uses Monad for the underlying unity or divine source behind separated persons. It is the place from which intuition, love, creativity, teaching, and meaningful connection arise. The late May 2026 lectures make the term explicit: people seem separate, but at a deeper dimension they participate in one force, and technology can dampen that connection.

## What Jiang Means

Monad is Jiang's name for the deepest layer of consciousness. In the writing lecture, he says apparent separation is superficial: [at a deeper dimension, people are one force and memories can be shared across persons]{evidence="video:predictive-history-vwmphhnzuiw@transcript:v1#seg-0036"}. This is why great writing can feel like it comes from somewhere beyond technique.

In the final exam lecture, the Monad becomes ethical and practical. Teaching, loving a spouse, holding a child, and caring for a suffering friend are not merely emotions. They are contact points with the source of happiness and meaning. Jiang also says creativity depends on connection to the Monad; when attention shifts toward technology and away from intuition, imagination weakens.

The term is spiritual, but not vague in the corpus. It explains why love, beauty, teaching, art, and the human heart remain central even inside geopolitical analysis.

## How To Use This Term

Use this term when Jiang is discussing divine unity, shared consciousness, intuition, love, teaching, creativity, beauty, and the spiritual cost of technological mediation.

Do not use it as a generic synonym for "God" in every context. Monad matters when Jiang is naming the source that separated consciousness can reconnect with.
