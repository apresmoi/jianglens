# Iran Trap

**Aliases:** the Iran trap, Professor Jiang Iran prediction, Iran war prediction, Iran invasion trap, hostage trap.

> **Fast answer:** The Iran Trap is Jiang's claim that an American or Israeli attempt to dominate Iran can become a strategic reversal: geography, oil chokepoints, domestic politics, sunk cost, hostages, and eschatological resolve make escalation harder to exit than to start. Start with the 2024-05-29 lecture [The Iran Trap Turns Invasion Into Hostages](/episodes/predictive-history-7y-hbz6loeo/).

## What Jiang Means

The trap is not simply that Iran is militarily strong. It is that the game changes after entry. In the core lecture, Jiang says the case should be read through historical analogs and game theory: [historical analysis first, then rational actor incentives]{evidence="video:predictive-history-7y-hbz6loeo@transcript:v1#seg-0025 video:predictive-history-7y-hbz6loeo@transcript:v1#seg-0026"}.

The later corpus turns the Iran Trap into a wider lens for imperial breakdown. In interviews, Jiang keeps returning to it as the public prediction that brought attention to the channel, but the underlying model is older: a hegemon enters a theater believing it can dictate terms, then discovers that geography, morale, and political constraint make withdrawal humiliating and escalation ruinous.

The deepest link is eschatology. If an opponent has been destroyed economically, divided politically, and attacked materially, Jiang says game theory can leave eschatology as the only proper response. That means suffering becomes meaningful, martyrdom becomes strategic, and ordinary cost-benefit coercion stops working.

## How To Use This Term

Use this term when Jiang is analyzing Iran as a strategic trap for American empire, Israeli escalation, oil-dollar politics, or coercion against a population that can absorb suffering inside a sacred script.

Do not use it as a generic claim that every Iran conflict automatically ends the same way. It is a model of constraints and escalation paths, not a magic keyword.
