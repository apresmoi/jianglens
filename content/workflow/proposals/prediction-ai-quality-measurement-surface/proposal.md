## Decision

Do not promote the December 9, 2025 AI-quality forecast until the project has a measurement rule.

The narrow candidate is:

> On December 9, 2025, Jiang predicted that ChatGPT or public AI-assistant quality would decrease over the next two years because models had reached the limit of clean internet data and were increasingly training on AI-created outputs, making answers less relevant and more confusing.

This proposal defines the measurement surface for a future ledger review. It does not decide whether the forecast has resolved.

If the project later wants to test the forecast, it needs a rule that separates Jiang's actual claim from adjacent claims about AI company finances, white-collar layoffs, censorship, model-release cycles, embodied data grids, and religious AI control.

This is synthesis rather than historical backfill. The corpus-impact audit still shows older missing sources, but no fresh source handoff is waiting, recent Plato work has already been impact-heavy, and PR #803 left this exact measurement gap as the next useful Prediction mutation.

## Source Claim

The source gives a date, horizon, mechanism, object, and symptom.

- **Source date:** December 9, 2025.
- **Horizon:** "next couple years" and "next two years."
- **Object:** AI in general first, then ChatGPT specifically as the public limits test.
- **Mechanism:** AI scrapes internet information, needs clean data to keep producing convincing answers, has reached the limit of internet data, and increasingly trains on AI-created data (`video:interview-lhssc-w0bk4@transcript:v1#seg-0128`).
- **Expected symptom:** ChatGPT quality decreases; answers become less relevant and more confusing as users talk with it more (`video:interview-lhssc-w0bk4@transcript:v1#seg-0129`).
- **Restatement:** ChatGPT/AI degrades over time, even if it still causes white-collar layoffs (`video:interview-lhssc-w0bk4@transcript:v1#seg-0131`).

The host's segment at `video:interview-lhssc-w0bk4@transcript:v1#seg-0130` restates the hypothesis as diminishing returns and the end of Moore's-Law-like progress. That can help interpret the conversation, but the ledger wording should rely on Jiang's own segments.

## Proposed Ledger Test

Use a three-part test.

### 1. Object Discipline

Primary object: **ChatGPT as experienced by ordinary public users**, because Jiang names it as the "big limits test."

Secondary comparison object: **public AI assistants generally**, only if a future review explains why ChatGPT-specific product changes make the wider assistant surface the fairer test.

Do not silently switch the object to:

- private frontier benchmarks;
- enterprise-only systems;
- "AI" as a civilizational or religious concept;
- robotics, sensors, chips, digital ID, or digital currency;
- labor-market adoption.

### 2. Symptom Discipline

Primary symptoms:

- relevance of answers to user prompts;
- confusion or incoherence in answers;
- usefulness over sustained conversation;
- degradation over repeated use or over time.

Secondary symptoms, allowed only as context:

- hallucination rates;
- benchmark scores;
- user satisfaction and churn;
- product restrictions, safety refusals, or monetization changes;
- cost and latency constraints.

Do not count any disliked answer as a miss or hit. The test must ask whether the answer surface became less relevant, more confusing, or less useful in a way plausibly connected to the data-quality mechanism.

### 3. Mechanism Discipline

The forecast's mechanism is **data-quality degradation through exhausted internet data and AI-created training data**. A future ledger review should ask whether later evidence supports that mechanism, or whether observed changes are better explained by another mechanism.

Alternative mechanisms that must be separated:

- safety tuning or censorship;
- deliberate product simplification;
- model routing to cheaper systems;
- cost pressure, subscription economics, or data-center scarcity;
- legal restrictions or licensing walls;
- adversarial prompting;
- user adaptation and rising expectations;
- embodied-data expansion through sensors, chips, digital ID, or payments.

If ChatGPT or public AI assistants become worse for reasons not tied to data-quality exhaustion, the ledger should record a partial/mixed result rather than a clean confirmation.

## Status Rules

Use these status rules if the candidate is later promoted.

| Status | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Active | Before December 9, 2027, unless later Jiang sources explicitly revise or withdraw the forecast. |
| Confirmed | By the two-year horizon, public ChatGPT/assistant use shows a broad decline in relevance/confusion/usefulness, and the best explanation plausibly includes synthetic-data or clean-data exhaustion. |
| Partial | The answer surface worsens, but the cause is unclear or appears to be cost routing, policy restriction, monetization, or product design rather than data exhaustion. |
| Miss | Public ChatGPT/assistant quality broadly improves or remains stable by the horizon, especially if relevance and usefulness improve despite synthetic-data concerns. |
| Reframed | Jiang later narrows the object, changes the horizon, or shifts the main mechanism from data exhaustion to embodied data, attention command, religion, or political control. |
| Unresolved | Evidence is too uneven, private, product-specific, or unavailable to score without pretending precision. |

## What Not To Do

Do not make a ledger item that says only "Jiang predicted AI would get worse."

Do not treat layoffs as proof that AI improved or degraded; Jiang explicitly separates job replacement from economic improvement.

Do not treat body chips, digital ID, or child tracking as confirmation of the quality forecast. They are the proposed escape route from the data problem.

Do not create standalone AI Quality Collapse, ChatGPT Decline, Synthetic Data Collapse, or AI Eats Itself pages from this measurement proposal.
