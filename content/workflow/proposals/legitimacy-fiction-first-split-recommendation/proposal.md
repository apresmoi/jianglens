## Parent Boundary

Keep **Legitimacy Fiction** as the parent for the inheritance problem.

The parent should answer one diagnostic question: how does a political invention survive its founding crisis and become the world later people must inhabit?

The parent keeps compressed gateway examples when the active mechanism is:

- a name, office, title, ritual, debt, rule, mercy practice, public scene, or text making a crisis transferable;
- multiple actors needing the fiction enough to repeat it, fight inside it, or inherit it;
- later people treating the fiction as binding reality rather than as a temporary speech act;
- a political order using inheritance to survive founder death, scandal, battle, election, fiscal emergency, or institutional collapse.

The parent should not keep absorbing every full biblical, Roman, American, financial, or imperial example. It should retain enough examples to teach the inheritance mechanism, then route mature submechanisms to children or neighboring pages.

## First Child Boundary

**Scripture, Identity, And Imperial Authorization** should own the narrower mechanism where writing and imperial permission edit political identity into an inheritable text-world.

The child is not:

- a David page;
- a Bible page;
- an Israel page;
- a religion page;
- an empire page;
- a general poetry or story-control page.

The child owns the path from crisis writing to public memory:

1. A political crisis needs more than force, secrecy, or temporary spin.
2. Writing makes the crisis durable enough to be taught, argued over, ritualized, or remembered.
3. Imperial authorization can turn return, temple, law, purity, and public reading into identity management.
4. The authorized text-world can outlive its sponsor and give the managed people a script for later independence, return, or refusal.
5. The same form can be recognized and reused by later empires or national projects.

That boundary preserves the parent while giving readers a sharper place for David's apology, Cyrus's return policy, Ezra/Artaxerxes authorization, temple-law-purity identity editing, and scripture as political memory.
