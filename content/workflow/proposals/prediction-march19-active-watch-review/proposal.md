## Source Trail

### Losing Gambler / Early Kark Formulation

Jiang first frames the war through fog and sunk cost. Because nobody can cleanly ascertain who is winning, the American side is stuck in a losing-gambler posture: if it quits, the loss becomes real. He then says Trump will double down, take "Cork/Kark Island" easily, and try to secure the oil (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0010`). The next segment adds mission creep and coalition pressure: Israel wants escalation, and GCC actors want the Iranian threat removed because drones or missiles could destroy Dubai's safe-haven aura (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0011`).

This is not yet a ledger-ready prediction. It is the initial branch statement: quick victory optics plus allied incentives create the next move.

### Quick Victory Optics And Coalition Pressure

Later in the interview, Jiang separates Trump's incentive from Israeli and Saudi incentives. Trump wants optics, surrender, oil control, and a better bargaining position when he goes to Beijing; Israel thinks longer term and wants Iran removed as a Middle East threat (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0049`). Jiang then names a coalition of Israeli and Saudi interests pushing Trump toward attack (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0050`).

This matters for Prediction because "Trump takes Kark Island" should not be scored as one personal-psychology claim. It is a player-map branch: Trump optics, Israeli threat removal, GCC safety, oil control, China bargain, and domestic political survival have different tests.

### Kark/Kharg Limited Operation Becomes Mission Creep

The clearest Kark branch begins at `seg-0052`. Jiang says Trump and America behave like a declining empire with hubris and desperation, underestimate Iranian resilience, and look for a knockout punch because a longer war damages the economy, polling, and markets. Kark/Karga/Kharg Island appears as the "quick solution" because Iran exports much of its oil through that point and a Marine Expeditionary Force is moving toward the region (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0052`).

Jiang then describes the plan: Marines bombard and land, secure oil deposits, Trump declares victory, and Iran is pressured to negotiate because the island is framed as an economic lifeline. But he immediately weakens the plan: the island is not safe, not as economically decisive as assumed, and Iran has coastal workarounds (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0053`).

The branch turns from operation into trap at `seg-0058` through `seg-0060`. Jiang says Iran can do more damage to the GCC than the island seizure does to Iran, oil could spike, and Iran may choose a long attrition war (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0058`). Once Marines take the island, they are exposed to drone and artillery strikes from the coast, so more Marines are required to secure the coast; once the coast is taken, mountain missile facilities force a much larger ground commitment (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0058`, `video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0059`). Jiang explicitly compares the escalation to Vietnam: a small initial Marine presence becomes hundreds of thousands of soldiers, mission creep, sunk cost, and eventually draft pressure (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0059`, `video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0060`).

This branch belongs primarily to No-Exit War and Chokepoint as mechanism. It belongs to Prediction only when scoring whether the named limited operation, troop expansion, draft pressure, or Vietnam-like escalation happened.

### No-Retreat Protection-Credibility Chain

The no-retreat branch starts from a different question: why not declare victory and go home? Jiang says that if Trump leaves the Middle East, the American empire collapses. Iran could demand GCC reparations or taxes after the damage, threaten Gulf infrastructure, and redirect GCC sovereign wealth funds from the United States toward Iranian rebuilding (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0066`, `video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0067`).

The protection story then spreads beyond the Gulf. Japan and South Korea see the United States unable or unwilling to defend the GCC and decide they cannot trust American protection, so they remilitarize and stop buying Treasuries. Middle Eastern and East Asian Treasury demand weakens; the dollar could lose value; debt crisis, depression, and street unrest follow. Europe then asks why it is still fighting Russia if America cannot defend allies (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0067`, `video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0068`).

The branch ends in the compressed no-retreat line: Russia becomes dominant in Europe, Iran becomes dominant in the Middle East, America implodes back into the Western Hemisphere, and therefore there is "really no possibility" of America retreating; it can only escalate (`video:interview-up9fnq23lsa@transcript:v1#seg-0069`).

This is adjacent to the Kark branch but should not be merged with it. Kark is a concrete limited-operation-to-ground-war sequence. No-retreat is a credibility and reserve-demand chain triggered by leaving or losing protection status.
