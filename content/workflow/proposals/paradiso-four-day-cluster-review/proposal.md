## Boundary Decision

Keep **Poetry** as the home for Dante's access method: imagination, paradox, ambiguity, language, vernacular rebellion, Beatrice as poetic-soul figure, terza rima, and posterity. June 18 strengthens Poetry because faith, hope, language, and form are how Dante reaches readers across exile and time. It does not yet require a new public Poetry child because the current Poetry page already carries Dante as access, quiet revolution, invasion, liberation, and Paradiso method.

Keep **Free Will** as the home for agency against obedience. The four-day cluster repeatedly distinguishes living will from external assignment, command, reward calculation, and static compliance. June 18's angels make this clearer: a being that cannot err, remember, imagine, or turn away is not Jiang's highest model of life. This should be future Free Will replacement/compression, not an Angels page.

Keep **Human Heart** as the home for fallibility, forgiveness, relation, and the moral weight of imperfection. The June 16-17 public compression already added the strongest promise/forgiveness and imaginative-entry material to `heart-forgiveness-requires-fallibility`. June 18 gives Adam, blame reversal, Beatrice, and human imperfection as sequel pressure, but the page is mature and should not receive another ordinary Dante append.

Keep **Dead World** as the home for static deadness, school-logic escape, and the mistake of treating perfected stasis as highest reality. June 16 gave the dead-eternity contrast; June 18 gives angelic stasis. A future public edit could compare these as dead perfection versus living fallibility, but only as replacement/compression with Free Will boundaries clear.

Keep **Religion As Administrative Filter** and **Bureaucracy** as secondary routing homes only. Indulgences, Peter's exam, Church authorization, Francis/Dominic decay, and theological office matter, but the cluster's active mechanism is Dante's poetic and free-will counterform. Do not let administrative material absorb the Paradiso concept.
