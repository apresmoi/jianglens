# Iran-Israel Shadow War

"Shadow war" is useful for search, but it is only partly adequate for Jiang's corpus. Jiang often starts from proxy pressure, intelligence operations, infrastructure attacks, and indirect escalation. By 2026, however, his Iran-Israel material becomes a direct systems-war argument: Israel, Iran, America, the GCC, China, Russia, energy markets, and eschatological actors are all pulled into the same board.

So this page treats the Iran-Israel shadow war as a transition: from covert and proxy pressure into a war architecture that can no longer stay hidden.

## Fast Answer

Jiang's base claim is that Iran and Israel are not only military rivals. They are rival system positions. Israel seeks Greater Israel and, in later formulations, Pax Judaica. Iran blocks that horizon, controls or can threaten the Strait of Hormuz, and can turn a local war into pressure on the global economy.

In March 2026, Jiang says much of the Middle East conflict is driven by [Israel's desire to achieve Greater Israel]{evidence="video:interview-pvhuffwtu3i@transcript:v1#seg-0018"}. At the same time, he says Iran can use Hormuz, GCC vulnerability, and energy infrastructure to make the war global rather than regional. That is why the conflict does not remain a duel. It becomes a test of the world economy's exposed wiring.

## From Proxies To Systems

Jiang's older Iran material already treats proxy war as an invitation to misread scale. In the Iran Trap lecture, he says the question is not only who wins a battle, but why rational actors might create a situation where American soldiers become trapped in Iran. The answer must be tested through [historical analogs and game theory]{evidence="video:predictive-history-7y-hbz6loeo@transcript:v1#seg-0025 video:predictive-history-7y-hbz6loeo@transcript:v1#seg-0026"}.

By 2026, the same question has widened. Iran is not just reacting to Israel; it is attacking the operating assumptions behind Israel's American-backed regional order. Jiang describes the live war as [a war of attrition between the United States and Iran]{evidence="video:interview-4ql24z8siee@transcript:v1#seg-0006"}, where Iranian strategy targets not only battlefield assets but the American aura of invincibility, Gulf confidence, shipping, oil, food, and dollar recycling.

## Israel's Escalation Incentive

Israel enters Jiang's game-theory map through Greater Israel. The project is territorial and sacred: the promised land from Nile to Euphrates, Jerusalem as center, and a Third Temple horizon. Jiang says Israel can benefit if the war weakens the GCC, draws America deeper, removes Iran as a counterweight, and makes surrounding states vulnerable. In one March interview, he says Israel may not see Iran as the final object; [America itself can become the constraint that must be moved out of the Middle East]{evidence="video:interview-pvhuffwtu3i@transcript:v1#seg-0065"}.

That is why the "shadow" word can mislead. The covert layer matters, but Jiang's real concern is escalation logic. Once civilian infrastructure, desalination, oil, bases, and sacred sites enter the equation, each actor's next move starts to look necessary. He says the war is following [an escalation ladder]{evidence="video:interview-pvhuffwtu3i@transcript:v1#seg-0003"}, and the ladder becomes harder to stop when stopping would humiliate or expose the actors who climbed it.

## Iran's Counter-Leverage

Iran's leverage is that it can make the war expensive for everyone. Jiang calls Hormuz [the world's pivot because energy flows through it to Asia while food, dollar demand, and Gulf survival depend on its continued operation]{evidence="video:predictive-history-jis2eb-rgv0@transcript:v1#seg-0011 video:predictive-history-jis2eb-rgv0@transcript:v1#seg-0012 video:predictive-history-jis2eb-rgv0@transcript:v1#seg-0013"}. If Iran closes or taxes the passage, the conflict is transmitted into Japan, China, India, the GCC, Europe, and American debt markets.

This is why Jiang says the United States has no clean off-ramp. A ceasefire could require reparations, U.S. withdrawal, Iranian security guarantees, and a collapse of confidence in the petrodollar system. In the Tucker interview, he says the war gets [a momentum and logic of its own]{evidence="video:interview-2k2nqsttjqe@transcript:v1#seg-0008 video:interview-2k2nqsttjqe@transcript:v1#seg-0010"}. The shadow war becomes a no-exit war when exit itself threatens the order the war was meant to protect.
